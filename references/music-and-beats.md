# Music and beat cuts

The AI cannot hear sound, so every audio judgment is made from waveform, spectrum, and timing analysis. At delivery you must remind the user to **listen to it in person**.

## 0. Where the music comes from: prefer composing with ElevenLabs from the storyboard

Off-the-shelf tracks rarely line up with a storyboard: the second version of Codefather used EDM and was called "the music is weird; the opening should sound puzzled." So ask the user first whether they have a paid ElevenLabs account (the composition endpoint is only open to paid plans; the free plan returns 402):

- **With a paid account**: per section 6, write the storyboard into a segmented composition plan. Write the opening mood (puzzled, tense, curious), the drops, the breakdown, the climax, and the ending into the narrative, generate 2 versions, then analyze and choose per the method in section 6.
- **Without**: go with the library below; for the pain-point opening section, the mood can be filled in with a loop generated by the ElevenLabs sound-effects endpoint (available on the free plan), but content generated on the free plan cannot be used commercially (see section 6).

## 1. Choosing a track

0. **The mood follows the narrative**: while the picture is describing a pain point, the music should be puzzled or tense (sparse, unresolved, no drums), and only at the brand drop does it enter a bright, high-energy section. A track that is high-energy from the first beat against pain-point visuals comes across as "weird."
1. Search 6–10 candidates by style (`music.py search music <tags...>`; Mixkit's driving tracks are mostly under the `edm` `funk` `electronic` `energetic` tags), and download them to `/tmp/music`.
2. `music.py analyze /tmp/music/*.mp3`: look at duration, BPM, the per-second loudness curve, and the guessed drop point; run `grid` on the finalists to check beat consistency.
3. Selection criteria:
   - **No slow warm-up**: enter the drop at 4–5 seconds (there need not be drums at the very start; the opening mood section can have none). For tracks with a long intro, cut in 1–2 bars before the drop (`musicOffset`). The first version of Codefather used a track with a 12-second intro and was called "slow-paced, music that doesn't fit."
   - **Has dynamics**: at least 2 drops across the film, with 1 breakdown (an energy dip) in the middle for an emotional turn. A track with a flat energy line makes the visuals feel flat too.
   - **The high-energy section is long enough**: use the `bars` table to confirm the main sections are all high-energy and that the ending section can be cut out separately to attach to the film's ending.
   - **Regular beats**: electronic tracks with consistency ≥ 0.9 are best for fast-cut beats.
   - **Right style**: don't use trailer music for chibi, don't use EDM for a keynote; "corporate video" background music is generally too tepid—use it with caution.
   - **Music follows the audience and the product, not the art style**: Yupi AI Navigation had a chibi-fish art style, and version 1 chose the childlike "Kids," which was called "the music doesn't really fit"; version 2 switched to an equally upbeat pop track and it was still "the background music isn't good." The audience is university students and professionals learning AI, and the product is a tool platform, so the final choice was the bright, inspiring "Better Times are Coming," with a breakdown and a build. A cute art style only means don't be too heavy; it doesn't mean a children's song.
   - **After two rejections, stop choosing on your own**: the AI cannot hear, so another blind pick in the same direction will most likely still be wrong. After the second rejection, widen the styles and produce 5–6 samples for the user to choose from.
4. **Listen and sign off**: cut 30 seconds from each of the 3–4 finalists (starting from the point that will actually be used) and normalize loudness:

```bash
ffmpeg -ss <start> -t 30 -i /tmp/music/1012.mp3 -af "afade=t=in:d=0.3,afade=t=out:st=28.5:d=1.5,loudnorm=I=-14:TP=-1" "out/music-candidates/A-track-name(style·BPM).mp3"
```

   Ask the user to listen and choose with a structured question tool, writing one line about each option's character. When the user says "you decide," pick per the criteria above and state the reasoning in the requirements confirmation.

   **When narration already exists, layer it onto the samples**: use the first 3–4 real narration lines, placed on each candidate at the timing they will have in the film, with the music ducked the way it will be in the film (`duck_curve`), making a 20–25-second "opening simulation." Yupi AI Navigation did this with 6 tracks and the user narrowed it to B and C in one round (the first two had been chosen directly without a listen and were both rejected). When the user asks you to choose between two, pick the one with more dynamics (with a breakdown and a build) and explain why.
5. Once chosen, copy it to `audio-src/music.mp3`.

## 2. Beat grid

```bash
python3 scripts/music.py grid audio-src/music.mp3                     # exact beat, bar, downbeat at the drop
python3 scripts/music.py bars audio-src/music.mp3 --beat 0.8955 --downbeat 14.38 --offset 2.88
```

- `grid` fits a period to low-frequency downbeats; the reported BPM may be half or double, so choose one as `beat` by ear (scene switches generally use the slower one, and fast cuts use half of it).
- **Trailer / hybrid-orchestration compositions have many hard hits and syncopations, and `grid` is often inaccurate** (the Fable composition gave 182 BPM with consistency 0.66). When consistency < 0.8, switch to `music.py tempo <file> --from 8 --to 46`: spectral-flux autocorrelation measures 120.19 BPM; it also lists the strongest low-frequency onset in every 2 seconds so you can find the cells whose onset spacing is exactly one bar and use them as bar lines, then verify the energy with `bars` (this time the first hard hit was at 4.05 seconds, so `musicOffset` was taken as 0.05 to land the hit at second 4 of the video).
- Decide the drop moment in the video `drop` (usually after the hook, 8–15 seconds), then `musicOffset = downbeat - drop`, i.e. video time t corresponds to track time t + musicOffset.
- `bars --offset` also prints the video timeline, used to confirm which energy section each scene falls on and whether the high-energy section is long enough.
- Put scene boundaries on bar lines (integer multiples of `bar`) or half-bar lines; fast cuts use a beat or half a beat, and dotted rhythms like `0.75 beat` also work.
- Write all time points into `timing.json`; put the in-scene step sizes (card interval, per-item reveal pacing) into `pace`, shared by the visuals and `mix.py`. When slowing down or speeding up the pacing, change only `timing.json` and both sides stay in sync.

## 3. Music entry and ending

- **Enter on a bar line**, not in the middle of a phrase (Vibe's first version started playing from second 2.88 of the track, right in the middle of a phrase, and sounded weird).
- Use `audiolib.build_in` for the build-up before a drop: cut the low frequencies, keep the volume low, and open gradually to full band within one bar, which feels more driving than a simple fade-in.
- The section before the drop can have no music at all, using only sound effects + `room_tone`, which makes the contrast at the drop stronger.
- Ending: the music "stops abruptly" about half a beat before the end logo → a hard hit + reverb tail → the track's own ending section (find the low-energy bar in the `bars` table and cut it out to attach).

## 4. Sound-effect alignment

- Every visual event needs a sound: transition whoosh, element appearance blip/pop, downbeat impact sub_boom, per-character typing keys, number rolling tick.
- Compute the time points from `timing.json`, and **never hard-code seconds in mix.py** (they will be off after a pacing change).
- Typing sound: `audiolib.keystrokes("2531.mp3")` cuts out single key presses and spreads them evenly across the typing time span by character count.
- Leave headroom between sound effects: downbeats 0.8–1.0, ordinary transitions 0.4–0.6, UI notifications 0.1–0.3.

## 5. Mastering and verification

- `Mix.master()` defaults to -14 LUFS and -1 dBFS true peak (works for Bilibili, YouTube, and TikTok).
- `python3 scripts/mix.py --env` prints the loudness every 0.5 seconds to confirm that the opening layers, entry point, build-up curve, drop jump, sudden-stop silence, and ending decay all match the design.
- Re-measure the loudness of the finished video with `check_video.py`.

## 6. ElevenLabs composition and sound effects

Preparation: install the official skills (`npx skills add elevenlabs/skills@music -g -y`, plus `sound-effects` and `setup-api-key`; installing is optional since `elevenlabs.py` calls the API directly), and have the user write `ELEVENLABS_API_KEY=...` into the workspace root `.env` (already in `.gitignore` in clone mode; in Skill mode confirm the workspace's `.gitignore` also ignores it; **do not let the user paste the key into chat**, and if they do, remind them to rotate it in the dashboard afterward). Then use `python3 scripts/elevenlabs.py quota` to see the plan and quota.

**Composition** (paid plan, `music_v2_5`):

1. Write the storyboard into `audio-src/el/score-plan.json`: `{"chunks": [{text, duration_ms, positive_styles, negative_styles, context_adherence}]}`, each chunk 3–120 seconds with the total equal to the film length. The first chunk's styles set the tone for the whole piece, so write 6–7 of them (including `instrumental`, BPM, style); write `vocals, singing, lyrics` in every chunk's negative field to avoid vocals; in `text` write only section labels and `{hints}`, not lyrics.
2. `python3 scripts/elevenlabs.py music score1 audio-src/el/score-plan.json`; generate 2 versions from the same plan (each takes about 10 seconds).
3. **The model does not strictly follow per-section durations** (the v2 series ignores duration): the intro planned for Codefather was 4.36 seconds but actually lasted 7.6 seconds, and the breakdown was 3 seconds late too. So measure the real structure with `grid` + `bars`, then **make the visuals follow the music instead**: use `musicOffset` to skip the excess intro (entering on a bar line) and move the scene boundaries onto the real drop and breakdown points.
4. Choosing: high beat consistency (≥ 0.95), an opening section that is genuinely quiet (low frequencies < -40 dB means no drums or bass), a clear breakdown and re-drop, and an ending with its own hard hit and close.
5. When it is too short, stitch on bar lines: use the `music` span table in `timing.json` (`[{at, from, to}]`), preferring to repeat the first 2 bars of a phrase (a phrase is 8 bars, counted from the drop downbeat), with a 30 ms crossfade at the stitch. Put the URL and CTA at the start of the repeated phrase so they land on a cymbal hit.
6. A composition's breakdown may be close to silence (-45 dB for 1–2 seconds). Fill it with a tension swell whose **peak is at the end**, with the peak landing on the re-drop.
7. Conversely, the model may not deliver a quiet section written into the plan at all (an interlude, an abrupt stop), leaving those bars high-energy anyway. No need to recompose: `audiolib.muffle(body, a, b)` low-passes that stretch to 420 Hz and drops it 5 dB, sounding like the music is behind a wall, then restoring full band immediately at the end right into the next hard hit (the 4-second "officials haven't spoken yet" passage in Fable was done this way).
8. When the film gets longer and the track is not long enough, do not recompose: write a `music` stitch table in `timing.json` (`[{at, from, to}]`, playing track `from~to` starting at video second `at`), repeating 2–3 bars from the middle, with stitch points on bar lines. The template's `mix.py` automatically stitches with `audiolib.splice` when it reads the `music` table (30 ms crossfade). After stitching, verify one thing: the climax scene's start time still lines up with the track's chorus entry.

**Sound effects** (the free plan works too): `python3 scripts/elevenlabs.py sfx <name> "<English prompt>" --dur <seconds> [--loop]`; the prompt is automatically recorded in `audio-src/el/prompts.tsv` for direct citation when writing the research notes.

- A one-shot "stretch of music" usually decays within 2 seconds and cannot fill a scene. When a continuous bed is needed, add `--loop` and write `continuous, no silence` and the BPM into the prompt.
- After generating each sound effect, measure its envelope (where the peak is, effective duration, tail): the same prompt gives a different result every time. The riser's peak was at the start in two generations; when you want "peak at the end," write explicitly `grows steadily and ends exactly at its peak`.
- The free plan limits concurrency, so sending several requests at once returns 429: generate them one by one (the script retries automatically).
- Do not layer pitched sound effects (like a rising "hmm?") on top of the composition, as the keys may clash; when the composition already conveys the emotion, layer only unpitched pops and impacts.
